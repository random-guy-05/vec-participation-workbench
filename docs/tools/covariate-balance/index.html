Interpretation and limits

For continuous covariates, the standardized difference is the comparison-minus-reference mean divided by the square root of the average of the two sample variances. For categorical covariates, it is calculated separately for each level as a binary proportion difference divided by its pooled Bernoulli standard deviation.

These are descriptive summaries, not hypothesis tests, causal estimates, automatic balance thresholds, or adjustment recommendations. A standardized difference does not prove confounding; a small value does not prove the groups are otherwise comparable. Check the study design, measurement units, missingness, and independent sampling structure.

Pasted rows are processed only in this browser tab. The tool does not upload, store, or fetch the data. Use public or synthetic values and keep direct identifiers out of the table.
